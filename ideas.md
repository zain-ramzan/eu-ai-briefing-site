# EU AI Briefing — Design Directions

## Three possible approaches

### 1. Civic Ledger

**Very Brief Intro:** A calm public-interest journal with the clarity of an official notice and the warmth of a well-edited newspaper. It makes the empty launch state feel like a deliberate editorial promise, not an unfinished product.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Quiet Observatory

**Very Brief Intro:** A spacious research-room aesthetic, using faint orbital lines and long reading columns to make policy monitoring feel patient and thoughtful.

**Probability:** 0.03

### 3. Signal Field

**Very Brief Intro:** A vivid information-design direction inspired by public transit maps and printed data sheets, designed to make policy signals easy to spot.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen approach — Civic Ledger

**Design Movement:** Contemporary European editorial design, borrowing the restrained authority of a public-interest journal and the compositional discipline of Swiss information design.

**Core Principles:** The site must feel evidence-first rather than promotional; editorial hierarchy must do the work instead of decorative panels; empty space must signal care and restraint; and every public-facing statement must visibly separate source, review, and publication.

**Color Philosophy:** Warm paper and soft stone create a human reading surface, while deep European blue signals traceability and public purpose. A small amount of sunflower gold is reserved for the “human approval” status so approval feels visible but never alarmist. The ownable signature color is **Ledger Blue** — a deep, ink-like blue that feels more like a trusted stamped record than a corporate interface.

**Layout Paradigm:** An asymmetric editorial sheet. A narrow left rail carries the site identity and publication rule; the main content column carries the briefing promise; a right-side vertical “process strip” shows the three-stage safety path. On small screens the rails flow into a reading order rather than becoming a crowded grid.

**Signature Elements:** A fine vertical rule that anchors the left editorial rail; small stamped status labels such as “Human approval required”; and a faint continental-dot field in the hero as a quiet reference to European scope.

**Interaction Philosophy:** Interactions should feel like turning a page or revealing a footnote: precise, understated, and never distracting. The one current action is deliberately non-publishing and explains that public briefings will appear only after human review.

**Animation:** On first load, the three process stages rise in sequence with a subtle opacity-and-vertical-motion reveal, 60ms apart. Links and buttons use a short 160ms color shift and a 0.97 active press. All non-essential motion is disabled for reduced-motion preferences. There are no looping or attention-seeking effects.

**Typography System:** Use `DM Serif Display` for high-authority editorial headlines and `Manrope` for compact, highly readable body copy and labels. Headlines are large but left-aligned; labels use tracked uppercase text; body paragraphs use generous line height and a modest reading measure.

**Brand Essence:** A public, human-approved EU AI policy briefing journal for European SMEs that translates official signals into practical reading without pretending to give legal advice. **Calm, rigorous, approachable.**

**Brand Voice:** Headlines are specific and quiet; calls to action explain boundaries rather than push conversion; microcopy avoids hype and promises only what the process can deliver. Example lines: “Official EU AI signals, read with care.” and “Nothing appears here until a person has checked the source.”

**Wordmark & Logo:** A blue circular seal made from three offset vertical reading marks that form a small open document and a central signal line. The wordmark uses a custom serif-and-sans lockup, never a default word-only treatment.

**Signature Brand Color:** **Ledger Blue — #123A70.**
